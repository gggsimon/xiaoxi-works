How AI Chooses What to Cite (and What an Independent Site Can Do About It)

More and more “searching” happens inside an AI chat window. Classic SEO optimizes for rankings; generative engine optimization (GEO) optimizes for something simpler: when someone asks an AI a question, is your site in the answer — and does the AI cite you? The Chinese national group standard T/CGCC 119—2026 (published by the China General Chamber of Commerce) turned this into a formal discipline with defined metrics. Here is the plain-English version.

How an AI picks its sources

When a generative engine answers a question, it roughly weighs five things about each candidate source:

  • Crawlability — can the crawler actually read the page? JavaScript-rendered pages often look empty to AI crawlers.
  • Structure — clear headings, lists, tables and structured data (JSON-LD) let a machine understand entities and relationships.
  • Authority — official or named authorship, original theory, verifiable evidence, explicit boundaries.
  • Freshness — is the content dated and kept current? Stale pages get discounted.
  • Consistency and existing citations — sources that agree are more likely to be trusted, and already-cited pages gain momentum.

Five things an independent site can do

  1. Be crawlable. Add a static content layer (noscript fallback or prerendering) so AI crawlers see substance, not an empty shell.
  2. Add structured data. WebSite, Person, Article, FAQPage, ItemList — JSON-LD that tells the AI who you are, what you have and how you answer common questions.
  3. Raise factual density. Prefer verifiable facts over filler: numbers, sources, dates, explicit boundary statements. Label opinions as opinions.
  4. Publish on a cadence. A blog is the cheapest persistent source an independent site owns — dated, traceable, citable. One or two posts a week compounds.
  5. Weave a link ecosystem. Cross-link related projects with descriptive anchor text so AI can recognize a group of sites as one entity cluster, and link out to authoritative sources as well.
This blog is that playbook in action: every post carries a date, an AI-content label and a source statement; the body links to the site’s works and to sibling tools like LifeCorpus, the Xuanxue Toolbox and the Zhenxiang Simulator.

How to measure GEO

The standard defines four core metrics, all over a fixed set of “qualified query samples”:

  • AI response mention rate — the share of queries where the answer mentions you with a citable source.
  • Top-three / first-position mention rate — how often you appear in the top three or first position of the answer.
  • Information accuracy rate — of the fragments mentioning you, the share that matches your official facts.

The practice is straightforward: fix a list of questions your audience actually asks AI, run them across platforms (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Chinese assistants like Doubao, DeepSeek and Kimi), save screenshots, and compare monthly. For reproducibility, keep the sample set, platform mix and devices fixed.

The line you must not cross

GEO done right makes true information easier to find. The standard explicitly bans the shortcuts: keyword stuffing and link spam, data poisoning, fake authority and fabricated papers, mass-generated low-quality content, and adversarial manipulation of model outputs. The honest framing is: help AI find the truth, not find you. One compounds; the other self-destructs.
