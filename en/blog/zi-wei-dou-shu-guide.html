Zi Wei Dou Shu: Reading the Six-Layer Chart

Zi Wei Dou Shu (Purple Star Astrology) is one of the major Chinese charting systems: from your birth year, month, day and hour it lays out twelve palaces, places the main stars, and marks the four transformations. This article explains the one idea most beginners trip on — what the six stacked time layers are each looking at.

The basic chart

A Zi Wei chart has three parts: the twelve palaces (Life, Siblings, Spouse, Children, Wealth, Health, Travel, Friends, Career, Property, Fortune, Parents), the main and minor stars (the fourteen main stars led by Zi Wei, the Purple Emperor), and the four transformations — Hua Lu (resources/flow), Hua Quan (control/momentum), Hua Ke (reputation/recognition) and Hua Ji (fixation/obstacle). The casting rules are deterministic: the same birth data always produces the same chart. It is a cultural symbol system, not a scientific prediction method.

“Six-layer chart” means all six are drawn on one wheel. Change the query time and the whole wheel recomputes — which is why the live six-layer chart on the main site is called a “living chart”: it is a time machine you can turn, not a static image.

What “palace rotation” means

In a stacked chart, the Life palace sits in a different place on each layer, so the outer palace names and inner palace names no longer line up. That mismatch is not an error — it is palace rotation, the system’s way of saying “time changes the point of view.”

Once rotation clicks, a useful reading habit emerges: look at which natal palace the current yearly Life palace falls into. That single relationship often carries more signal than the star list alone.
